How this world works

10 tiny neural networks live on this planet as students. Every lesson, exam and choice you see comes from real PyTorch networks whose weights change while you watch: no language model, nothing scripted. Source, tests and docs: github.com/andrewsavio/AI-World.

Are these AIs like ChatGPT or Gemini?

No, but they are built on the same basic idea: a neural network, which is lots of tiny "neurons" joined by adjustable connections. ChatGPT and Gemini have hundreds of billions of connections. Each student here starts with about 10–20, small enough for you to watch every connection change as it learns.

What are they learning?

Each subject is a map of blue and red dots, and the student must learn the rule that separates them, from examples alone. Levels 1–4 build up from straight lines to rings and checkerboards. From Level 5 the objective is quantum computing: a small exact simulator of two qubits produces the data, and the students learn superposition, interference, entanglement and Grover's search from measured outcomes.

How do they learn?

Guess → check → nudge. One lesson is one dot: the student guesses, compares with the true answer, then nudges every connection a little so the next guess is better (backpropagation with gradient descent, the same method used to train real LLMs). Watch it step by step in 🔍 Watch one lesson, next to the planet.

Who decides what they do?

They do. Each student has needs, a personality and likes it learns from experience: good outcomes (passing, progress, discoveries) make an activity more attractive next time. That is reinforcement learning (a second network trained with REINFORCE). Click a student to see why it chose what it's doing.

Honest limits

Each student maps a few numbers to a yes/no answer. It cannot read, reason about physics or invent anything new to humanity: its discoveries are real quantum mechanics that physicists already know, new only to the AI World. The students use no internet.

More: exams, getting stuck, the Quantum Lab, buildings, the seven levels, time and saving

Exams

Exams use dots the student has never seen before, so passing proves it understood the rule rather than memorising answers (a test set). Experiment data is noisy, so the pass mark is set just below what a simple reference method (copy the answer of the 7 most similar examples) manages on the same data.

When they get stuck

They can grow new neurons, read a paper another student published in the Library, or invent a new way of seeing the map at the Research Institute, such as "distance from the centre". The first to invent something gets a 🌍 World First.

The Quantum Lab

A student designs its own experiment: a small two-qubit circuit with two settings, plus a choice of what to measure. A confirmed experiment becomes that student's own subject. From Level 6 students search for circuits with clear patterns and share them; it counts as teaching when another student passes one. Subjects marked legacy come from an earlier version that used public web data.

Buildings

Coins from exams, promotions and discoveries found new buildings: a Data Centre (more lessons a second, upgradable), a Data Lab (more experiments of their own), a Lecture Hall and a Hall of Discoveries.

The seven levels

1 Foundations → 2 Shapes → 3 Curves & Logic → 4 Patterns (plus an experiment of its own) → 5 Qubits → 6 Entanglement & algorithms (plus a circuit another student learns from) → 7 Quantum research (teach others 5 times). Easy before hard, as in curriculum learning. Press 📜 Syllabus to see who is where.

Time and saving

The world runs on your computer's clock, so students sleep at night. At night the lesson panel replays the last real lesson. The world saves itself every 2 minutes and continues next time.

Tech stack

One HTML page (three.js, no build step) served by server.py (Python standard library), which also runs the brains in brain.py (PyTorch, CPU): a skill brain per student trained by backpropagation and a decision brain trained by REINFORCE. The quantum data comes from an exact two-qubit state-vector simulator. Automated tests run with pytest on GitHub Actions.
